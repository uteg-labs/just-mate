What we built in 24 hours
  • Native iOS & Android app (Expo dev-client): faceless onboarding with a live LLM interview, map, 1:1 Plans, match card, compass, post-meet, report; EN, PL & SK.
  • Bun + Elysia server: accounts and profiles in PostgreSQL, one WebSocket per phone with a 1-second matching loop, a compass relay that sends a bearing — never a position — and the Plans engine over seeded public venues.
  • A shared, typed protocol — written before code — used by both app and server; 170 automated server tests.
  • Our own matching model (PyTorch → ONNX) trained on 25,000 synthetic profiles scores Now; plans use the explainable score.

01 The problem

Loneliness, and the door

Loneliness touches about one in six people and is linked to an estimated 871,000 deaths a year. It is highest among young people — 17–21% of 13–29-year-olds — and lonely people are twice as likely to become depressed (WHO Commission on Social Connection, June 2025).

Matching is solved. Meeting is broken.

It breaks hardest for a lonely, maybe low person. Existing apps monetise attention spent on the phone: their incentive is the opposite of getting you outside. For someone who already finds it hard to go out, three barriers stand between them and a real conversation:

  1. Initiative. Low mood means low energy. Organising something — even choosing between options — is too much. "Find people now" assumes you are already out and already brave.
  2. The empty table. "What if nobody comes? What if I'm stood up?" One no-show confirms the story nobody wants me.
  3. Judgment. Photo-first apps turn meeting into an audition; chat turns it into weeks of performance before anything real happens.
"Apps solved matching. Nobody solved the door."

The product's job is not the first meeting, but the second, the third and the tenth: friendship takes roughly 50 hours together to go from acquaintance to casual friend (Hall, Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 2018).

Design consequence

Every mechanic in JustMate removes one of the three barriers. The phone's job ends where the conversation starts — the interface is the street.

Why existing formats fail

FormatFailure mode for a lonely person
Swipe apps (Tinder, Bumble)Photo-first judgment; chat purgatory; meetings are the exception, not the goal.
Scheduled dating (Breeze)Real meetings, but planned hours or days ahead — and dating only.
Stranger dinners (Timeleft)Proves strangers show up to a fixed plan, but on fixed weekly slots, paid per seat, anywhere in the city.
AI-matched groups (Pie)Small groups matched by a quiz, then a group chat before the event — the chat gate again.
Proximity dating (happn)Retrospective ("who you passed 7 days ago"), photo-first, chat-gated.
Ambient proximity (Highlight, Sonar)Always-on location, battery drain, empty rooms, safety gaps. Both are gone.
Activity platforms (Meetup)Needs an organiser and a group; big rooms are hard for the shy; nothing for "a beer buddy now".

One finding we build on: Timeleft's co-founder observed that "the more information you give, the more people don't show up". A faceless badge is the right amount of information.

01 The problem · why it is a health task

Connection is health.
It starts at the door.

JustMate is a health & wellbeing product for the loneliness epidemic. It treats nothing; it changes what people do — meet someone in person, move to get there, look up from the phone while they talk — and come back again.

WHO asks every country to make social connection a public-health priority. The everyday steps it names read like our spec — "putting away one's phone to be fully present in conversation", "joining a local group" — with cafés and parks as social infrastructure and walking as active transport.

Four pathways from an app to wellbeing

Move · activityEvery meeting starts on foot: Now matches only within a 5-, 10- or 15-minute walk; plans sit at a public venue within a 15-minute walk of both. 16 of the 44 Mate activities are sport or outdoors — running, climbing, padel, football, yoga, hiking, cycling, a walk.Light activity on the way, and a partner for sport today

How we will show it works — without health data

In the product · behaviour, never health
  • Real-world meetings per active user per week; repeat meetings with the same person or group
  • Movement: sport or outdoor share of meetings; minutes walked to meet M1
  • Time in app should not grow
Outcome study · research partner M1

Opt-in and anonymous: the 3-item UCLA Loneliness Scale and the WHO-5 Well-Being Index at sign-up and after 8 weeks, run by a Kraków university partner under its own consent and ethics approval. JustMate sees aggregates only.

Wellbeing, not medicine. No health claims (EU MDR), no health or mood data (GDPR Art. 9), no guilt mechanics (ch. 02). Psychologists, counsellors and GPs can recommend JustMate as a next step between sessions (social prescribing, ch. 12).
