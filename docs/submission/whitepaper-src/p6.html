10 Engineering

Protocol first, tests that read like the spec

Built in 24 hours by a team working in parallel. What made that possible: the contract was written before the code, a mock server replayed it so the app never waited on the backend, and the safety rules live in tests, not just in documents.

Quality gates

  • 170 automated server tests (Bun test) across pairing, offers, sessions, zones, matching rules, age ranges, Plans, report and block, profile validation, moderation, embeddings, onboarding fallbacks and the ice-breaker, the WebSocket handshake and localization.
  • Biome lint and TypeScript typecheck on every package; bun run lint && bun run typecheck && bun run test before every push.
  • Drizzle owns the schema with versioned SQL migrations; Better Auth owns its tables.
  • GitHub Actions: conventional-commit version bumps and release-on-tag; EAS builds of the dev client for both demo phones.

11 Business model

Venues pay. People meet for free.

Four principles: meeting is never paywalled; safety is never paywalled; those who earn from people going out — venues and events — pay most; premium sells convenience and reach, not access.

Revenue streams

StreamHow it works
Partner venues primaryCafés, board-game bars, climbing gyms, cinemas list plan slots for ~€39 a month and fund perks on check-in. They fill off-peak tables; their slots seed density. Partners see counts and check-ins, never identities.
Prepaid first roundAn optional €5 voucher at partner venues, sold as a €20 wallet top-up; JustMate keeps 15%. Doubles as a commitment device — prepaid people show up.
JustMate+€4.99 a month: host your own group plans (ID-verified hosts, public venues), travel mode, private plans for existing friends. Proposals and 1:1 invitations stay free.
Events & festivalsLicensed official zones and plans for an event, €500–2,000 each.
Later: B2B2CEmployers onboarding relocated staff, universities onboarding international students, city wellbeing programmes.
Rejected on purpose

"Five free connections, then pay"

It paywalls the north-star event and charges lonely people for the one thing they came for. It rewards rationing meetings — the swipe-app model we position against. Limits exist in JustMate, but for safety, not revenue.

Never

Ads in the meeting flow. Selling attention. Selling data. A paid safety feature. JustMate's success metric decreases phone usage, so attention is not our product.

12 Launch, density & competition

Plans lower the density bar

The 2012 proximity apps died of empty rooms. JustMate's answer to cold start is structural: a new city opens with plans; the Now loop lights up as density grows.

Now · needs people in the same minute
~9
people searching at any evening minute in a district of 500 active users who each search 30 minutes a week — split across two modes and a dozen intents. A Now match is rare.
Plan · needs people over two days
~16
people per intent over the next 48 hours if 40% of the same 500 are open to plans. A 1:1 plan needs one of them; a group of four fills easily.

Go-to-market

  • Single-density launches: one campus, one festival, one district — anywhere the glow is populated from day one.
  • Event mode: festivals and conferences pre-seed official zones and plans for a known, opt-in crowd.
  • Venue partners: board-game cafés, breweries and climbing gyms host plan slots — density and revenue at once.
  • Newcomer channels: campus societies, expat communities, HR onboarding, international-student offices.
Referral channel · psychologists and counsellors M1

Referral, not treatment

Psychologists, counsellors, student counselling centres and GPs see lonely clients every week, and often suggest exactly what Plans make easy: small, concrete, scheduled activities. JustMate is a next step to recommend between sessions — the way they'd recommend a climbing club. Referring people to community activity is established practice: in England, GPs referred 1.3 million people to social prescribing in 2023 (UCL, 2025).

Without health data. A referral kit (leaflet + a QR code per practice); the client signs up like anyone else — no "patient" flag, nothing reported back. Installs are counted per practice and never stored on a profile, because "referred by a therapist" is health data (GDPR Art. 9). No health claims and no clinical integration keep JustMate outside the EU MDR. Pilot: 3–5 practices and a university counselling centre in Kraków.

13 Metrics & roadmap

Measured on the street, not the screen

North star

Completed real-world meetings per active user per week

Plan and Now combined.

Mission metric · loneliness

Repeat meetings

Share of active users who met the same person or group at least twice in 30 days. Repetition is how friendship starts.

Anti-metric, watched on purpose

Time in app should not grow

Engagement is measured on the street. The phone's job ends where the conversation starts.

ActivationShare of new users who attend a first plan or meeting within 7 days of signing up
Plan healthProposal acceptance (proposed → both in); invitation take rate (sent → someone's in → confirmed); group fill rate; show-up rate ≥ 80% of confirmed people; stood-up rate (checked in, nobody else came) under 2%
Safety guardrailsVanish rate under 10%; reports per 1,000 meetings; median time to review a report under 24 h; notification opt-out rate
MovementShare of meetings that are sport or outdoors; minutes walked to meet — aggregate only, never a track
Outcome (research only)Opt-in, anonymous 3-item UCLA Loneliness Scale and WHO-5 Well-Being Index at sign-up and after 8 weeks, with a research partner — aggregated, never stored on a profile

14 Demo scope, honesty & sources

What's real, what's canned

Rule for every sentence in this document, the deck and the description: it describes what the demo does, or it is labelled production path.

Real in the build
  • Accounts, profiles, onboarding with live LLM text (fixed samples without a key)
  • Zone glow from live positions, with k-anonymity
  • Mutual match delivered live to both phones over WebSocket, with haptics
  • Every hard gate, incl. age segregation; the explainable score; our own model scoring Now for real accounts
  • Compass from the real magnetometer; bearing + bucket relay; Vanish; We met; post-meet with an ice-breaker; Report → block, two-report pause
  • 1:1 Plans: proposals from real profiles, accepted on both phones; invitations with several times; the confirmed plan with its compass
  • Account export and delete
Canned, and labelled as such
  • Demo-mode positions (indoor GPS) on a scripted track relative to the stage
  • Nine ghost wanderers adding map density — they never match or join plans
  • A demo time-skip that starts a plan two minutes after it is proposed
  • The list of 18 public venues in Kraków, hand-tagged by the team
  • The selfie liveness check (simulated; production path)
  • The learned model's training labels: synthetic and rule-based, not real meetings

AI-use disclosure

AI tools were used for ideation support and boilerplate; the core idea, mechanic and solution are the team's work. The product itself uses AI: an LLM (OpenAI gpt-4o-mini) interviews the user and writes their vibe line, matching traits and an ice-breaker line after meeting; OpenAI moderation screens every saved profile; OpenAI embeddings encode profiles for our own matching model, which scores Now matches for real accounts. Plan proposals and the stage demo use an explainable compatibility score. The model was trained during the event on synthetic profiles (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small embeddings, PyTorch).
